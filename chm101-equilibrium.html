Course Overview

This course introduces students to the fundamental principles of chemical equilibrium and their application to chemical reactions. It examines the dynamic nature of equilibrium, the factors affecting equilibrium systems and the quantitative relationships used to describe equilibrium conditions.

Students will explore equilibrium constants, reaction quotients, Le Chatelier's principle and the effects of changes in concentration, pressure and temperature on chemical systems. The course also introduces acid-base equilibrium and solubility equilibria.

Course Aim

To provide students with a sound understanding of the fundamental principles of chemical equilibrium and the ability to apply equilibrium concepts to chemical systems.

Learning Objectives

Students completing this course should be able to:

  1. Explain the concept and characteristics of chemical equilibrium.
  2. Distinguish between homogeneous and heterogeneous equilibria.
  3. Write equilibrium constant expressions for chemical reactions.
  4. Calculate equilibrium constants and equilibrium concentrations.
  5. Explain the relationship between reaction quotient and equilibrium constant.
  6. Apply Le Chatelier's principle to predict changes in equilibrium systems.
  7. Explain the effects of concentration, pressure and temperature on equilibrium.
  8. Apply basic acid-base equilibrium principles.
  9. Solve quantitative problems involving chemical equilibrium.

Course Content

  1. Introduction to Chemical Equilibrium
  2. Dynamic Nature of Chemical Equilibrium
  3. Law of Mass Action
  4. Equilibrium Constants (Kc and Kp)
  5. Reaction Quotient (Q)
  6. Homogeneous and Heterogeneous Equilibria
  7. Le Chatelier's Principle
  8. Effects of Concentration, Pressure and Temperature
  9. Acid-Base Equilibria
  10. Solubility Product and Precipitation Equilibria
