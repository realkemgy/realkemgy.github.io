Course Overview

This course introduces students to the fundamental physical and theoretical principles of molecular spectroscopy, with emphasis on the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with atoms and molecules. It explores the quantisation of molecular energy levels and the physical mechanisms responsible for spectroscopic transitions.

The course covers the theoretical foundations of microwave (rotational), vibrational, electronic (UV-Visible), Raman, magnetic resonance, nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), electron spin resonance (ESR/EPR), Mössbauer, and photoelectron spectroscopy.

Particular attention is given to quantum mechanical models, molecular energy transitions, spectroscopic selection rules, molecular symmetry, and the interpretation of spectral features. Students will develop an understanding of how different spectroscopic techniques provide complementary information about molecular structure, bonding, and electronic properties.

Course Aim

To develop a comprehensive theoretical understanding of the fundamental principles of molecular spectroscopy and the physical processes governing the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter.

Learning Objectives

Students completing this course should be able to:

  1. Explain the fundamental principles of spectroscopy and radiation–matter interactions.
  2. Understand the quantization of molecular rotational, vibrational, electronic, and magnetic energy levels.
  3. Explain the theoretical basis of major molecular spectroscopic techniques.
  4. Describe and apply spectroscopic selection rules and transition probabilities.
  5. Understand the influence of molecular structure, symmetry, and bonding on spectroscopic behaviour.
  6. Apply fundamental equations and mathematical models to explain spectroscopic phenomena.
  7. Interpret basic spectral features and relate them to molecular properties.
  8. Compare different spectroscopic techniques in terms of their underlying physical principles and chemical information.

Course Content

  1. General Introduction to Spectroscopy
  2. Microwave Spectroscopy
  3. Vibrational Spectroscopy
  4. Electronic Spectroscopy (UV-Visible)
  5. Raman Spectroscopy
  6. Magnetic Resonance Spectroscopy
  7. Nuclear Magnetic Resonance (NMR) Spectroscopy
  8. Electron Spin Resonance (ESR/EPR) Spectroscopy
  9. Mössbauer Spectroscopy
  10. Photoelectron Spectroscopy
