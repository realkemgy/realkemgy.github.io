What is molecular spectroscopy?

Molecular spectroscopy examines the interaction of electromagnetic radiation with matter. The frequencies and intensities observed in a spectrum provide evidence about molecular energy levels and can be used to investigate molecular structure and behaviour.

Why study it?

Different regions of the electromagnetic spectrum probe different molecular energy changes. Rotational, vibrational, electronic and magnetic-resonance methods therefore provide complementary information. Learning to connect a measured spectrum with the underlying molecular process is central to interpreting spectroscopic data.

Core ideas to keep in view

1
Energy is quantisedMolecules occupy allowed energy states.
2
Transitions involve energy differencesRadiation is absorbed or emitted when allowed transitions occur.
3
Selection rules matterNot every energy-level transition produces an observable spectral line.

Suggested approach

A study path that builds understanding

Move from the physical basis of spectroscopy to individual techniques, then practise interpreting the information contained in spectra.

01 · Build the foundationReview electromagnetic radiation, quantised energy and molecular energy levels.
02 · Understand transitionsStudy rotational and vibrational behaviour before moving to electronic transitions.
03 · Compare techniquesConnect IR and Raman activity with molecular properties and selection rules.
04 · Interpret evidenceUse spectral patterns, calculations and worked problems to reason about molecules.
