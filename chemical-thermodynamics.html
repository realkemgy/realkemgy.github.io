Course Overview

This course introduces students to the fundamental principles of chemical thermodynamics and their application to chemical and physical processes. It examines energy transformations, the properties of thermodynamic systems, and the conditions governing spontaneity and equilibrium.

Course Aim

To develop an understanding of the fundamental laws and principles of thermodynamics and their application to chemical systems and processes.

Learning Objectives

Students completing this course should be able to:

  1. Explain the fundamental concepts and laws of thermodynamics.
  2. Differentiate between thermodynamic systems, surroundings and boundaries.
  3. Explain the concepts of heat, work, internal energy and enthalpy.
  4. Apply the first law of thermodynamics to chemical processes.
  5. Explain entropy and the second and third laws of thermodynamics.
  6. Determine the spontaneity of chemical and physical processes.
  7. Apply Gibbs free energy to chemical reactions and equilibrium.
  8. Solve basic thermodynamic problems using appropriate equations.

Course Content

  1. Introduction to Chemical Thermodynamics
  2. Thermodynamic Systems, Properties and Processes
  3. Heat, Work and Internal Energy
  4. The First Law of Thermodynamics
  5. Enthalpy and Thermochemistry
  6. The Second Law of Thermodynamics
  7. Entropy and Entropy Changes
  8. The Third Law of Thermodynamics
  9. Gibbs and Helmholtz Free Energy
  10. Thermodynamic Criteria for Chemical Equilibrium
